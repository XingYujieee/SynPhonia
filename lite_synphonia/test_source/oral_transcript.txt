Good morning. We are Group 23, and this Research Presentation studies the synergy of frequency-domain and spatial-domain filtering.
Our core challenge is simple: MSE often looks precise, but it may not reflect real visual quality.
We first diagnosed the mixed noise model with the FFT spectrum. The image contains stripe noise and salt-and-pepper noise.
So our four-step strategy is spectrum analysis, LPF cleanup, spatial refinement with Median or NLM, and evaluation with MSE and SSIM.
Stage one focuses on LPF parameter tuning. The key variable is the cutoff frequency D0 for removing periodic stripes.
D0 at 70 under-filters, D0 at 50 over-smooths, and D0 at 60 gives the best balance with MSE 0.0299.
Stage two tests baseline spatial filters: Mean, Gaussian, and Median.
Gaussian is worst at 0.0301, Mean degrades edges, and Median reaches 0.0178 and handles impulse noise best.
Then we built a hybrid pipeline by chaining LPF with spatial filtering.
The best traditional combination is LPF plus Median, with MSE 0.0294 and stronger robustness on mixed noise.
At this point the numbers create a visual illusion. The lowest MSE does not automatically mean the best-looking restoration.
That is why we questioned whether the apparent winner on paper actually preserves local detail and texture.
The scientific weakness of MSE is that it only measures pixel intensity error.
Median lowers MSE but over-smooths texture, so we need a metric that respects structure instead of only brightness differences.
Our upgraded evaluation uses SSIM, which compares luminance, contrast, and structure.
With SSIM the ranking reverses: Median drops to fifth place, while LPF plus Mean rises to the top with SSIM 0.601.
Classic filters still share a bottleneck: isotropic smoothing and weak edge-awareness.
Gaussian shows this clearly with SSIM 0.507, because uniform smoothing destroys important structure.
That leads to our core algorithm, NLM or Non-Local Means.
NLM searches for similar patches, assigns weights by patch similarity, and preserves edges better than simple local averaging.
Our final validation combines LPF with NLM as the ultimate solution.
LPF plus NLM achieves the highest SSIM, 0.607, while keeping a strong MSE near 0.0293.
So the final conclusion is structure first: collaborative frequency-domain and spatial-domain filtering is more reliable for mixed noise restoration.
